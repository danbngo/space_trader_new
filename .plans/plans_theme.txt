talking to timmi makes me think...

whats the point of the space map with all the shit going on on it? sure its cool, but what does it really add?

im starting to remember that game design is 50% about cutting away, rather than adding. AI is good for one thing, but the other..
still requires good old fashioned hard work, foresight, planning, understanding of aesthetic/theme

statue of david wouldnt all of a sudden be cooler if they added things like a cool sword and a wolf at his heel

basically like... what is the EXPERIENCE im envisaging for the player when they're playing my game?
is he whipping out his phone every 5 minutes to make a decision?
is he in front of a desktop clicking and typing endlessly and using hotkeys?
is he calm or is he excited/sweaty?
etc. etc.

what major existing well-mapped, well-understood class of game does my game strongly resemble?

is it a menu driven game ala seedship or the OG space trader?
  ^^ for this we need to cut the flying around part, go back to random encounters, have a side <> side combat system
  the emphasis becomes about planning, decisions and understanding the game world and how the stats interact (econ simulator)
is it a full spectrum roguelike you have to control every moment you're playing? like dwarf fortress?
  ^^ for this we need to do what tim said, make a chunky astronaut guy who can land and explore, and go tile based
  here the emphasis is more on roleplay, experiencing the game world and novel discrete interactions
is it a twitch game where you control shit on the map and click click click?
  ^^ nah
is it a 3d space shooter where the emphasis is on combat and novel physics interactions?
  ^^ this is mostly just for "cool" factor and to obviate the need to think about certain things because "realism". i dont think
  its an actua plan.
