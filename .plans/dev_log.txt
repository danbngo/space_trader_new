been a bit down in the dumps, because i had to cut a lot of content from my game. it doesnt feel like the same game i was trying to build now.
is it closer to the og space trader? oh hell yeah, but that wasn't REALLY what i set out to do... i wanted to have something more like
EVE online, single player, text based (minimal graphics for the plebs)

and then i rightly pointed out to myself: that won't sell. that won't make for a good gameplay experience. of the 2 points, the latter hits
harder. at some point i forgot there was this thing called players.

but not to wallow in that- the point is, i was depressed over that. but i think i understand how to fix it now... i had to return to the source.
what DOES space trader feel like to play? through the lens of "what do i care about as a player?"

and from there it's obvious.

creating a game is not about throwing things together. that's the same as throwing chocolate or steak mignon into applesauce. 
are they good things? yes. do they go together good with applesauce? fuck no. will they confuse the shit out of the person eating the
applesauce? yes. 

like it or not, game dev is a lot like cooking. there are fucking RECIPES. you don't alter the recipe much if you want people to understand
your food... ENJOY your food. there's a time and place to experiment, and that's done cautiously, one thing at a time usually. not a ...
wild zany fricasse of every conceivable ingredient and spice. that's not originality, that's the same as throwing a bunch of paint together
you ALWAYS get the same gray soupy mess if you do that. and people been making grey soupy messes since time immemorial.
even fucking ARCADE GAMES IN THE 70s had games that were too conceptual, trying to do too many things at once.

in other words, the process of discovering fun.. involves trial and error. any deviation (and i do mean any) off the beaten path runs the risk
of destroying fun, the same way you can destroy a recipe, or a painting, with one fatal stroke. one thing that doesn't need to be there.
and sure, people will still eat or, or look at it, and probably even enjoy themselves if you only took the one wrong turn.
or added the one unnecessary ingredient. or the one feature that didn't really enhance any of the others.

you can get away with one. but two? three? then you're in gray soupy mess territory. anything that isn't stroking that fun center in the brain
is a gray soupy mess. it's postmodern art. getting high on its own farts instead of trying to deliver something beautiful and real and...
SPARING.

that's the word of the day - sparing. 

having said that, life sucks if you JUST stay on the beaten path, JUST stick to the known recipes, because how the fuck do any new recipes
ever get created? but it takes HUMILITY. it takes understanding the audience, what a game IS, what gamers are, what the process of enjoying
a game is. how RARE true enjoyment, true art, true perfection, a true enhancement or breakthrough really is in this world of gray soup.

that's PART of the joy, really.. fun is elusive. just like inspiration is inexplicable. they are 2 sides of a coin, and the interestingness
of life is how to appease one without fully pissing off the other, like having a wife and a mistress. (lol)

what is all this circling around?

you had a good idea to cut, but now that you cut, you should really just raze everything to the ground and start over with a new philosophical
principle in mind... 

1: "what does not immediately impact and ENHANCE the core loop, doesn't need to be in the game".

but there is a light that is the beneficent cousin of that darkness... 

2: "your job as the dev is to impact and ENHANCE the core loop, refract its light, as much as is humanly feasible".

THAT's why the civ stats need to go. not necessarily because they're bad on their own. but because they GET IN THE WAY of something much better.

the question is -- how do i make the player care about this feature (without bludgeoning him over the head with it)
the answer -- tie it into the core loop in a way that's thematic and feels right. feels like it belongs. or at least has a place.

for instance, governments - what do they effect? news. stats. and what do those effect? encounter weights. price and demand.

so basically you have a stat, that effects 2 systems, that in turn effect OTHER stats, that finally effect the core loop.

that is wrong. 

3: "every system that exists must interface as DIRECTLY with the core loop as is humanly feasible".

in an rpg, the sky is basically the limit. your core loop is "living the life of an epic fantasy adventurer", and so anything that an epic fantasy
adventurer might do - forge weapons and armor, go on quests, fight demons, "level up", train, recruit allies - fits in the core loop.

but space trader is no fucking rpg. it's a simulator. a trading, exploring, spacefaring, pirating... but mostly TRADING... simulator. 
hence the fucking name "SPACE TRADER"

would a merchant in a particularly unscrupulous or roughshod time period do all of the above? YES. even mining, possibly yes.
and buying stuff, and visiting banks... yes and yes. getting rank-ups so he can get more rare goods and beat the tax system... YES.
hire mercenaries and shit to guard his cargo? yes.

getting fucking neuro implants and weapons so he can fight people up close? not really bro. just cruising around the system saying hi to
random ships? not really. those things COULD HAVE BEEN IN THE GAME, but did they fit in the core loop? not really.

and get what i'm saying, core loop does not equal theme. i get that it sounds like i'm describing theme, but i'm not. we're talking about the
fun. the ephemeral, ineffable, hard to explain but you know it when you see it - just like you know great food when you taste it - fun.
part of fun is having extras, but mostly, it's about having a predictable understandable unit of something you've seen enough times to 
understand - and hence, enough to appreciate a twist of novelty added on top.

why do people salt meat? because meat tastes kinda salty, and salt enhances it even more. black pepper too. certain things complement each
other. cyber modules, etc would've made sense IN A ROLEPLAYING game where you're a space ADVENTURER. this is not a rpg, this is a sim.

the point i'm making... is a little divergent from everything i've said so far. i've made it sound like this is all a process of deciding
what to put in, and what not. and to some extent that's true. cyber modules, no way did those belong whatsoever. the 2d realtime map SUPER
didn't belong at all. really complex news that "draws you in" roleplay style doesn't belong.

but the point is, the things that DO belong? it's not just about having them there and having them effect things (ie, effect a system,
which effect stats, which effect enocunters, which in turn effect the player). at that point, does anyone remember or care what all
the upstream effects were?

no, things need to have IMMEDIATE impact on the core loop if at ALL possible. yes, in skyrim you can collect ingredients, to forge
your weapon, to then enchant your weapon, which you can THEN fight enemies with - and if fighting enemies was the core loop, somewhere along 
the line this would've been too much. 

but remember - the core loop is BEING A FANTASY HERO, and he would do all those things - and it's enjoyable to do all those things
and not be fighting the entire time.

but, what skyrim did RIGHT was not make items have durability, and not make them have all these intense granular stats... because we -want-
the player focused on the experience of adventuring. the epic stuff. the hero stuff. not the grind-y stuff. 

and in space trader, WE WANT THE PLAYER FOCUSED ON MERCHANT-Y stuff. agonizing over trade routes, costs, prices. figuring out just how much
hired muscle he needs to make it to the next system. talking his way out of fights, but fighting when he absolutely must. deciding
when to smuggle and when to keep his nose clean. etc.

IF A SYSTEM DOESN'T CONTRIBUTE TO THAT OR ENHANCE IT IN SOME WAY, IT SHOULDN'T EXIST - 
BUT
IF A SYSTEM IS NOTICEABLY MISSING WHICH COULD CONTRIBUTE TO THAT OR ENHANCE IT IN SOME WAY, IT -SHOULD- EXIST NO MATTER WHAT!

this is --very-- similar to the concept of "your food lacks soul." chili wouldnt taste the same without some dark, bitter, acidy, musky pepper.
granted, it probably took someone a long time to figure that out, but once they did. chili became 10x better.

and players can detect this, at times. how many people wanted a horse mod for morrowind, or good ranged weapons? and sure enough, in the 
next iterations the devs delivered because as a player you can FEEL the absence of these things that feel like they SHOULD exist in the game.

and this is exactly what i'm trying to drill home and what you got wrong previously... you need to be playing the game, or a similar game,
and feeling out what should and shouldn't be there and develop on the basis of THAT... because with THAT, the need to make everything
about the core experience is obvious. from a player's perspective - the guy EATING the actual food - it's always obvious when something's
overwhelming, or muddled, or missing a piece, or "lacking soul".

an example-
in this game, there's governments. WHO THE FUCK CARES ABOUT THOSE. in space trader you barely notice them at all. what do you do?
you go straight to the market or the shipyard.
so guess what? the GOVERNMENT SHOULD BE the market or shipyard. ie, a "primitive" type planet only allows barter. a communist planet
refuels/stocks you for free, but also taxes you. a corporatist planet ... idk, does something zany market related. the democracy is the 
"vanilla" one. etc, etc.

that is an inifinitely better mechanic than having stats, that effect stats, that... and you know it. you fucking KNOW IT.

and the point is not just that one little example, it's the whole thing. the whole thing could benefit from you going in with your magnifying
lens and asking, what would i have built this like if i'd been THINKING from the start of this shit about how we make mechanics that
-force the player to care, and if he then finds them annoying, odds are it's a bad mechanic
-tie into the main gameplay loop, experience, concept DIRECTLY. not through adjacent stats/systems.
-each do something UNIQUE and PALPABLE.

cargo types need this question to be asked. the recruiting system needs this question to be asked. ship types and stats need to be examined.
the news system itself needs to be examined. everything needs to be turned upside down and given the "FORCE the player to care about this
IMMEDIATELY and if he still can't/doesn't want to, get rid of it, but also make sure everything that a real space trader (like a movie about
an epic space trader, not a literal space trader using the bathroom and doing mundant shit all day) WOULD be forced to care about has
some in-game equivalent" treatment.
